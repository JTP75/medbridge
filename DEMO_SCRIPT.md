# MedBridge Demo Script — "47"

**Format:** ~2:30 video, first-person researcher voiceover (VO) over B-roll.
**Design intent:** The tool/UI appears only during the turn and payoff
(~1:00–2:05) as a single hero moment. Everywhere else, the story — not the
interface — carries the demo. The protagonist saves the day; MedBridge is
what makes it possible.

## Premise

A rare-disease researcher studies **DIPG** (diffuse intrinsic pontine
glioma) — a rare, fatal pediatric brainstem tumor. Her funding renews only if
she can prove enough cases exist to study. Her own hospital has too few. No
one else can share patient records without exposing identities, so no one
can even tell her what they have.

**MedBridge's insight:** don't ask hospitals for records — ask for counts. A
count can't identify anyone, so hospitals can finally answer. That's not a
side-feature bolted onto the story — it's *why* the payoff number is
possible at all: privacy is the researcher's secret weapon, not an obstacle
worked around.

## Why This Arc Works

- Both hackathon-relevant obstacles resolve in one arc: the **count** kills
  the isolation problem (can't find who has the data); **counts-not-records**
  is *why* the compliance wall falls (hospitals can safely answer) — no
  separate beat needed for it.
- The "3 vs. 47" moment is literally true to how rare-disease imaging data is
  distributed across hospitals, so it doubles as a demonstration of the
  small-cohort-suppression policy without feeling like a feature tour.
- UI/tool screen time is deliberately minimized (~1:00–2:05 of a 2:30 video)
  — the story, not the interface, is the protagonist's arc.
